# Market data

Exchange rates, economic indicators, activity data, company filings and equity quotations are read in loaders from public APIs, validated with Zod and cached per source. News and product prices have their own guides: [rss-sources.md](rss-sources.md) and [product-price-feed.md](product-price-feed.md).

All modules live in `app/lib/`.

## Exchange rates

`blue` is the dashboard's highlighted rate and the grid shows `oficial`, `bolsa`, `contadoconliqui` and `cripto` (`HIGHLIGHT_CASA` and `GRID_CASAS`). A live response without `blue` is rejected. One missing a grid rate is cached for only 5 s.

- **Variation**: the live sale price against the last close before today (Argentine calendar day), so a close already published for today is never compared with itself. History keeps 90 calendar days and the sparkline draws the last 30 points. History is optional: if it fails, the dashboard still renders with the variation as `s/d` (*sin datos*).
- **Stale fallback**: when a live request fails, the last successful response is served for up to 15 minutes from the moment it was fetched, labelled with that time. Failed attempts do not extend the window. Past it, the rate cards show as unavailable while the history charts load independently.

## Indicators

`/indicadores` groups its cards by subject (`INDICATOR_GROUPS` in `indicators.ts`): prices (monthly, annual and REM expected inflation), interest rates (30-day deposits, BADLAR, TAMAR, personal loans), dollar and external sector (reserves, retail and A3500 wholesale dollar, country risk), money (monetary base, year-over-year private M2) and indexation units (UVA, UVI, CER, ICL). Every card but country risk is attributed to, and read from, the BCRA; country risk is attributed to Ámbito and read from ArgentinaDatos, because the BCRA does not publish it.

- **Source**: BCRA Estadísticas Monetarias v4.0, no key. The documentation is the Swagger page linked from `https://www.bcra.gob.ar/apis-banco-central/` (spec: `https://www.bcra.gob.ar/archivos/Catalogo/Content/files/json/estadisticas-monetarias-v4.json`); the PDF manual is `https://www.bcra.gob.ar/archivos/Catalogo/Content/files/pdf/principales-variables-v4.pdf`. The BCRA limits traffic per client IP address and publishes no figures.
- **Latest values**: one request to the catalogue, `GET /monetarias?categoria=Principales Variables&limit=3000`, whose rows carry `idVariable`, `ultFechaInformada` and `ultValorInformado`. Each card names its `bcraVariable`. Rows are validated one by one, so a malformed or missing row blanks only its own card. The catalogue's latest date for UVA is today, although the series itself already lists the coming days.
- **Histories**: `GET /monetarias/<id>?limit=3000&offset=<n>[&desde=YYYY-MM-DD]`, newest first in `results[0].detalle` with the total in `metadata.resultset.count`. A `limit` above 3,000 answers 400, as does a `desde` after today. The first page is read, then the remaining pages in parallel; a series needing more than four pages fails instead of truncating. Monthly inflation (`27`, since 1943) is one request; the deposit rate (`12`) is read from 2000-01-01, in three. The series starts in 1985; the deposit-vs-inflation comparison covers the months since 2000.
- **Series choice**: the ids are the BCRA's own (`1` reserves, `4` retail dollar, `5` A3500, `7` BADLAR, `12` 30-day deposits, `14` personal loans, `15` monetary base, `25` private M2, `27`/`28` inflation, `29` REM, `30` CER, `31` UVA, `32` UVI, `40` ICL, `44` TAMAR). BADLAR and TAMAR each have two series with the same description: `7` and `44` are "En porcentaje nominal anual" and `35` and `45` "En porcentaje efectivo anual", per the catalogue's `unidadExpresion`. The cards use the nominal ones, like the deposit rate.
- **Units and dates**: values are shown as reported, in the series' unit (percent, pesos, millions of dollars or pesos, index), never rescaled. Each card states its own date: reserves and the monetary aggregates usually lag about a week, and the rates several business days.
- None of the rates is presented as a monetary-policy rate. The BCRA no longer announces one, and the app substitutes nothing for it.
- The BCRA catalogue and country risk are cached and fail independently. `/api-status` reports them as two rows, "BCRA (Estadísticas Monetarias)" and "ArgentinaDatos (riesgo país)".
- Each card discloses the series' period, unit and source, links the series itself, and copies those details on click.
- `/resources/inflation-history` serves the monthly inflation series to the client components that compare purchasing power and deposits with inflation. A successful response is public (one hour in browsers, one day in shared caches); an unavailable one is `private, no-store`.

## INDEC (canastas, salarios, IPC)

Three tables on `/indicadores`, after the cards, read INDEC's own API: the canasta básica, the wage index and the IPC by division, category and region.

- **Source**: Catálogo API-INDEC, `https://api.indec.gob.ar/v1/<service>`, in beta. Every data request needs `Authorization: Bearer <token>`; the token is a Worker secret, `INDEC_API_TOKEN` (see [cloud-finance-setup.md](cloud-finance-setup.md#configuration)). The catalogue (`/api/datasets`) and the OpenAPI spec (`/api/openapi?v=2`) are public. Queries use PostgREST syntax (`select`, `order`, `limit`, `offset`, `column=op.value`, `or=(…)`), and a response holds at most 1,000 rows. Rate limits are not published. Reuse requires citing "Fuente: INDEC" and the service consulted; each table links its service's catalogue page.
- **Rows**: the three services share a long format, one row per `codigo`, `tipo_de_indicador` (`v_m` monthly change, `v_ia` yearly change, the value itself), `region` and `periodo` (`YYYYMM`). A value replaced by one of INDEC's conventional signs (`///`, `s`, `...`) arrives with `valor` null and the sign in `valor_null`, and is shown as `s/d`. Rows are validated one by one, so a malformed row only blanks its figure.
- **Requests**: one per service, filtered by `periodo=gte.<four months back>` and to the columns and indicators shown, which keeps each response under 400 rows (about 50 KB for the IPC, the largest). A response that reaches 1,000 rows fails instead of truncating. Each table shows the latest month with data in its window.
  - `valorizacion_canastas_cba_cbt`: CBA and CBT per adult equivalent (`codigo` `CBA`/`CBT`, with their `v_m` and `v_ia`) and for household 2 (`CBA H2`/`CBT H2`: four members, 35, 31, 8 and 6 years old). Gran Buenos Aires only, the region INDEC values them for.
  - `indice_salarios`: `codigo` `0` (general level), `110` (registered private), `120` (public) and `200` (unregistered private), `v_m` and `v_ia`. The real yearly change divides by the national IPC's yearly change for the same month, read from the IPC response; without it, that column is `s/d`.
  - `ipc_nacional`: `region=Nacional` (general level, the twelve COICOP divisions, and the categories core, regulated, seasonal, goods and services) or `nivel=Nivel General` (the six regions), `v_m` and `v_ia`.
- **Failure**: the three services are cached and fail independently; a missing token fails all three without a request. `/api-status` reports them as one row, "INDEC (Catálogo API-INDEC)".
- **CPU**: parsing and building the three responses takes about 4 ms on a cache miss and well under 1 ms from the cache.

## Economic activity (EMAE)

The panel shows year-over-year activity, the seasonally adjusted monthly change and the last 12 observations, converted from fractions to percentages. It reads INDEC's series through the Datos Argentina API and falls back to the CSV export of the same series when the API fails. The panel is unavailable only when both fail.

## Acciones

`/acciones` has three panels, chosen with `?panel=`: `acciones` (BYMA quotations: the disclosure, the index strip and market state, the CEDEAR table and the leaders table), `empresas` (SEC EDGAR filings) and `calculadora` (the CEDEAR theoretical-price calculator). Everything a panel shows, its disclosure included, sits below the tab line, so the header above it never changes between panels. The `acciones` panel exists only while `EQUITIES_BYMA_ENABLED` is on; see "The BYMA gate" below. An unknown or switched-off panel redirects to the default: `empresas` while BYMA is off, `acciones` while it is on. The former `cedears` and `lideres` panels are unknown ids and redirect the same way; the two tables keep the `#cedears` and `#lideres` anchors. The calculator is never the default. With a single panel available, the page shows no panel switch; today there are always at least two.

The page is named for what it shows: "Empresas" while BYMA is off and "Empresas y acciones" while it is on (`accionesLabel` in `acciones-panels.ts`), in the sidebar, the heading, the page title, the error state and the `/docs` section. The path stays `/acciones` in both states.

### The BYMA gate

BYMA's website terms require its prior and express authorization to use its content, and that authorization has not been obtained. `EQUITIES_BYMA_ENABLED` is therefore `"false"` in `wrangler.jsonc`, and it stays off until BYMA authorizes the display. Only the literal string `"true"` turns it on (`isEquitiesBymaEnabled` in `acciones-panels.ts`); a missing or mistyped value keeps it off.

While it is off:

- no code path calls BYMA: the `/acciones` loader calls no `equities.server.ts` function, and `/api-status` neither runs `checkEquitySources` nor lists a BYMA row;
- the page offers no `acciones` panel (index strip, market state, CEDEAR and leaders tables, disclosure, BYMA footer line), and its ids redirect as described above;
- the top-bar search does not offer the two BYMA panels. The search index is built on the client, so the terminal layout's loader passes the flag down (`loaderData.equitiesBymaEnabled`, never revalidated, since it only changes with a deploy), and `/docs` reads it in its own loader to describe only what is shown.

While it is on, the BYMA panels behave as described under "BYMA panels", and the Empresas panel is the third tab. Route tests (`acciones.loader.test.ts`, `api-status.loader.test.ts`) pin both states.

### Empresas (SEC EDGAR)

The list shows the issuers of a static table in `company-filings.ts` (ticker, Spanish name and sector of Argentine companies with shares or ADRs listed in the US) with the latest annual report (form 20-F, 10-K or 40-F, amendments excluded), its period and filing date, linked to the document on sec.gov. `&empresa=<ticker>` shows one company: its 12 most recent reports (`isListedFiling`: 20-F, 10-K, 40-F, 10-Q, 6-K, 8-K and their amendments; insider-holding Forms 3 to 5, SD and registrations are left out because they crowd out the reports) and four figures from its latest annual report (revenue, profit, total assets, equity). An unknown ticker redirects to the panel without it; a lower-case one redirects to the upper-case ticker.

- **Source**: EDGAR, the SEC's public filing system. Information published on sec.gov is public and may be copied and redistributed. The attribution line is "Fuente: SEC EDGAR." A recorded TGS response is in `company-filings.server.fixtures.json`.
- **Identity**: the table holds tickers only. The CIK is resolved at run time from `https://www.sec.gov/files/company_tickers.json` (an object keyed `"0"`, `"1"`, … of `{ cik_str, ticker, title }`), and a company is shown only when its submissions' `tickers` array lists the expected ticker; a ticker missing from the file, or a CIK that does not list it, reads as "no identificado" rather than as another company's data. No CIK is written in the code.
- **Filings**: `https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK<10 digits>.json`, whose `filings.recent` block is a set of parallel arrays, newest first. The arrays are zipped up to the shortest required one, empty `reportDate` and `primaryDocDescription` strings read as missing, and the document link is `https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/<cik>/<accession without dashes>/<primaryDocument>`.
- **Figures**: `https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK<10 digits>/<taxonomy>/<tag>.json`. The four `ifrs-full` tags (`Revenue`, `ProfitLoss`, `Assets`, `Equity`) are asked first, and the `us-gaap` ones (`Revenues`, `NetIncomeLoss`, `Assets`, `StockholdersEquity`) only when all four answer 404; a 404 means the company does not report that tag, not an outage. For each tag the latest annual fact comes from a 20-F, 10-K or 40-F with the latest period end (a 350 to 380 day duration for revenue and profit, a single date for assets and equity), ties going to the latest filing date and then the unit with more facts. `alignToOneReport` then keeps only the figures of one report (the latest period end, from the latest filing that carries it), because each tag's latest fact can be years apart (TGS's `ProfitLoss` stops at 2017) and restated pesos from different reports are not comparable. The table states that report's period end, form and filing date once and shows each figure as filed with its unit: no currency conversion and no growth rates, because Argentine issuers restate for inflation. A tag missing from that report shows `s/d` (banks usually have no `Revenue`). A filing's description is the filer's own `primaryDocDescription` (in English) when it says more than the form name, such as `MATERIAL FACT`; otherwise the form's Spanish name.
- **Requests**: every request sends `User-Agent: TuEconomia/1.0 (<support address>)` from `support.ts` and `Accept: application/json`, as the SEC's fair-access policy requires. Keep a URL out of it: the SEC answered 403 ("Request Rate Threshold Exceeded") to the same header with the repository URL in it. Request starts are spaced 125 ms apart in each Worker isolate (at most eight per second, under the SEC's ten), outside the 8 s timeout. Each request is cached by key (`tickers`, `submissions:<cik>`, `concept:<cik>:<taxonomy>:<tag>`); there is no stale fallback.
- **Subrequests**: a cold list costs one ticker-file request plus one submissions request per issuer (14 in all for the current 13 issuers); a cold company view costs the ticker file, one submissions request and four concept requests, or eight for a US-GAAP filer (at most 10). `company-filings-budget.test.ts` pins both against the real table and the Worker's 50-subrequest limit.
- **Independence**: each issuer resolves on its own; the list is unavailable only when the ticker file cannot be read or every issuer fails. In the company view, the filings and the figures stream separately, and a failed concept names its figure while the others stay visible.
- **API status**: every SEC request is reported as one "SEC EDGAR" row, `partial` when some fail or are unanswered while others succeed. `/api-status` loads the issuer list inside its 10 s window.

### Calculadora (CEDEAR theoretical price)

`?panel=calculadora`, available in both flag states. The visitor types the underlying's price in dollars, the conversion ratio and, optionally, the CEDEAR's market price in pesos; the page shows no quotation of any security. The only figures the application supplies are the CCL (`contadoconliqui`) and MEP (`bolsa`) sale prices from Dólar API, the same values the dashboard shows. The logic is in `cedear-calculator.ts` (client-safe, with tests); the component is `CedearCalculator.tsx`.

- **Formulas**: theoretical price = underlying × rate ÷ ratio, where the ratio is CEDEARs per underlying share (20 for `20:1`, 0.5 for `1:2`). With a market price: implied rate = market × ratio ÷ underlying, its gap against the reference rate = (implied ÷ reference − 1) × 100, and the premium = market − theoretical, also as (market ÷ theoretical − 1) × 100. The two percentages are equal by construction; the page shows the gap once and the premium with its peso amount, and says so.
- **Rate**: the sale price (`venta`), as everywhere the app quotes a single value for a rate (variation, gap against the official rate, charts). The converter's buy-side convention for USD→ARS answers a different question (what selling dollars pays) and does not apply to valuing a security. The rate is prefilled from Dólar API with the quote's time, and a "sin actualizar" note while the 15-minute stale snapshot is shown. The page names Dólar API once, in its footer source line; the calculator itself names the rate ("CCL de venta"). The visitor may always type another value; the field's helper says when the value was typed, the origin line shows the current quote, and "Usar esta cotización" goes back to it. Changing CCL/MEP drops a typed rate when the new reference has a live value. When the rates are unavailable, the field starts empty and an amber notice asks for the value.
- **URL**: `precio` (US$), `ratio` (`20` or `1:2`), `referencia` (`mep`; `ccl` is the default and omitted), `tc` (a typed rate; absent while the live one is used) and `mercado` (pesos). The loader normalises them like `empresa`: typed forms are rewritten canonically (`150,25` → `150.25`, `20:1` → `20`, `MEP` → `mep`), invalid, zero, negative or out-of-range values are removed, repeated ones collapse to the first, and the result is a redirect. Other panels redirect without these parameters, and the calculator without `empresa`. Fields write the URL on blur (the reference toggle on change), with `replace`; each field resyncs only when its own parameter changes, so a typed-but-unblurred value survives unrelated query changes. `shouldRevalidate` skips the loader when only these parameters change.
- **Parsing**: a comma is the decimal separator; dots group thousands (`1.234,5`, `1.234.567`). A single dot is read as a decimal point (`150.25`) unless it is followed by exactly three digits with a non-zero integer part (`1.500`), which is ambiguous and rejected. Bounds: underlying US$ 1,000,000, rate ARS 1,000,000, market price ARS 1,000,000,000, each side of the ratio 100,000; at most ten integer and six decimal digits. Incomplete or invalid input shows `s/d`; nothing is filled in.
- **Loading and failure**: the loader streams `getLiveRates()` (`exchange-rates.server.ts`), which shares the dashboard's cache, stale fallback and health record but makes no ArgentinaDatos request. A failure resolves to the unavailable state, never to an error, and the page retries in the background (`useFailedDataRetry`) while the rates are unavailable, stale or missing one of the two. `?preview=unavailable` produces that state without calling Dólar API. The panel never calls BYMA or the SEC. The footer reads "Fuente: Dólar API."
- **Not included**: no ratio table, no tickers and no prices of real securities. The page says the ratio is set by each programme's issuer and may change, and that the result ignores commissions, taxes, the bid/ask spread, trading hours and dividends.

### BYMA panels

Shown only while `EQUITIES_BYMA_ENABLED` is on. The CEDEAR panel (ETFs included) and the S&P Merval leaders panel are each ranked by peso traded amount, above a strip with the S&P Merval, the S&P BYMA General and whether the market is open. The loader streams the strip and only the selected panel; the Empresas panel does not show the strip. The CEDEAR table shows the 30 most traded (`CEDEAR_ROW_LIMIT` in `acciones-panels.ts`); the leaders table shows all 20 with name and sector. Names and sectors come from a static table in `equities.ts`, because BYMA sends `description` empty; an unlisted symbol shows its ticker.

- **Source**: BYMA Open Data, the free public API behind BYMA's own site: no key, 24 hs settlement prices, delayed about 20 minutes. It is not a documented or contracted API for this project: BYMA can change it without notice, and the request headers and envelopes follow the open-source `openbymadata` client rather than published documentation. Outside market hours every list comes back empty. An empty list is rejected as a failure, so the panels read as unavailable whenever the market is closed. The values are shown as reference, attributed "Fuente: BYMA Open Data, con demora"; see "The BYMA gate" for why the panel is off.
- **Species pairing**: a CEDEAR trades as up to three species, e.g. `AAPL` in pesos (`ARS`), `AAPLD` in MEP dollars (`USD`) and `AAPLC` in cable dollars (`EXT`). A suffixed symbol is paired only when its base exists in `ARS` and its own currency matches the suffix, so a ticker that merely ends in `C` or `D` keeps its own row. The `cedears` and `etf` lists are merged, with the CEDEAR list winning a duplicate.
- **Implied rate and decomposition**: the implied rate is the peso last divided by the dollar-species last (the conversion ratio cancels out). It uses the `EXT` species (CCL) when it traded today and falls back to the `USD` species (labelled MEP). The day change splits as `(1 + pesos) = (1 + dollar species) × (1 + implied rate)`, each against its previous close. When neither dollar species traded, or a previous close is missing, the rate and the decomposition show `s/d`; nothing is filled in.
- **Proxy limitation**: the dollar-species change stands in for the underlying's move. It is not the price on the underlying's home market, and most dollar species are thin, so the proxy can lag or miss the day's move. The page says so.
- **Caching and stale fallback**: each of the five requests is cached for 2 minutes with an 8 s timeout. BYMA answers 400 at random under load, so a 400 is retried once, within the same 8 s. When a refresh fails, the last successful list is served for up to 15 minutes from its fetch, labelled with that time; `market-open` is never served stale, because an old open or closed flag would contradict the clock. The panels are independent: one failing request leaves the others visible, and the CEDEAR table stays available while either the `cedears` or the `etf` list loads, naming the missing one.
- **API status**: the five requests are reported as one "BYMA Open Data" row: `partial` when some succeed while others fail or have not answered yet (including requests still in progress when the page's 10 s window closes), `stale` when a failure was covered by a snapshot.

## Shared behavior

- **Caching** (`promise-cache.server.ts`): concurrent requests for a key share one upstream fetch, and a rejected promise is evicted. A failed key backs off for 5 s, doubling to a 30 s cap, and callers get the same error meanwhile; a success resets it and a key quiet for 5 minutes is forgotten. The cap stays under the 30 s first delay of the browser's automatic retry (`use-failed-data-retry.ts`, doubling to 5 minutes, only while the tab is visible and idle), so a retry always reaches upstream. At most 256 failure records are kept.
- **Timeouts** (`upstream-timeout.server.ts`): every upstream request has a hard deadline that settles even when the fetch ignores its abort signal.
- **Streaming**: slow or optional data, such as the dashboard quotations, streams in behind a loading state, so a slow upstream never blocks the page.

## API status

`/api-status` lists each source's last result: `not-checked`, `fetched`, `cached`, `stale`, `partial` or `failed`, with the last success and failure times. It runs the application's own data paths instead of a separate probe, within a 10 s limit, and a source still unchecked when the limit passes shows as `failed`. The health is kept in memory, so it describes the Worker instance that answered.
